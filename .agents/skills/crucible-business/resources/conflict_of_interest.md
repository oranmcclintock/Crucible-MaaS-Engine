# Navigating the Conflict of Interest Conversation

As an apprentice/employee starting a B2B side business, having a proactive conversation with your employer about Conflicts of Interest (COI) is the most mature and legally protective step you can take. 

Handling this correctly ensures that **you** retain 100% ownership of your Intellectual Property (the Crucible Engine) and that your employer cannot claim it belongs to them.

Here is your strategic playbook for that conversation.

## 1. Before the Meeting: Audit Your Contract
Before you speak to HR or your manager, read your employment/apprenticeship contract carefully. Look for these two specific clauses:
*   **Non-Compete / Moonlighting Clause:** Does it explicitly forbid you from taking on secondary employment or running a business? (Usually, it only forbids businesses that *compete* with your employer).
*   **IP Assignment Clause (The Dangerous One):** Does it say "Any intellectual property created during the term of employment belongs to the company"? If so, you need a written carve-out.

## 2. The Three Golden Rules of Side Businesses
To legally protect your IP, you must strictly follow these rules immediately:
1. **Never use company hardware:** Never write a single line of Crucible Engine code on your work laptop. Never use your work phone for sales calls.
2. **Never use company time:** Only work on your business on weekends, evenings, or approved leave. 
3. **Never target company clients:** Do not use your employer's internal CRM or client list to snipe targets. 

## 3. How to Frame the Conversation
When you speak to your manager or HR, **do not over-explain or apologize.** Frame it as a transparency measure for a personal skill-building project that happens to be monetized.

**The Script:**
> *"Hi [Manager/HR], I wanted to proactively disclose that I am doing some independent consulting on the side outside of my working hours. It focuses strictly on legacy database migrations. I have reviewed our company’s policies and confirmed this does not compete with our core business or clients.*
>
> *I am doing this entirely on my own time, using my own personal equipment. I just want to ensure everything is above board and recorded transparently with HR to prevent any future perceived conflicts of interest."*

## 4. The Goal: Get It In Writing
A verbal agreement means nothing if a legal dispute arises 3 years from now when your engine is generating £500k a year. 
*   After the meeting, send an email summarizing the conversation: *"Thanks for chatting today. As discussed, I am pursuing an independent side-project regarding database migrations on my own time and equipment. Confirming we agreed there is no conflict of interest with my current role."*
*   Save their positive reply to your personal (non-work) email address.

## 5. What if your employer is a System Integrator?
If your current employer *is* a mid-tier SI that does data migrations, you have a direct conflict of interest. In this scenario, you cannot operate the business secretly. You have two options:
1. **The Spin-Out:** Ask for written permission to build tools they don't currently offer, explicitly defining that you own the IP.
2. **The Internal Pitch:** Pitch the Crucible Engine to *them* as an internal tool, but negotiate a licensing fee or promotion rather than giving it to them for free.
