# CME Group: Outside Activity Disclosure Draft

This document provides exact, HR-friendly answers for your CME Group outside activity disclosure. The goal of these answers is to clearly demonstrate **complete separation** between your side business and CME's core business (global derivatives, financial exchanges, and clearing).

## Standard Questionnaire

**1. Time commitment: Will it interfere with my ability to perform at CME?**
> **Draft Answer:** "No. The outside activity is strictly limited to weekends and non-core hours. The expected time commitment is 5-10 hours per week maximum, ensuring no interference with my responsibilities at CME."

**2. Competition: Is this with a competitor or potential competitor?**
> **Draft Answer:** "No. The outside activity involves providing backend IT data migration services (specifically legacy database profiling) to IT System Integrators (SIs) and public sector agencies. This is completely unrelated to CME Group's business as a financial exchange and derivatives marketplace."

**3. Industry overlap: Is it related to the same industry as CME?**
> **Draft Answer:** "No. The target industry for my outside activity is UK Public Sector IT (e.g., government IT infrastructure upgrades). It does not involve financial services, trading, or clearing operations."

**4. Misuse of information: Is there a potential for misuse of CME Group information when performing outside activity?**
> **Draft Answer:** "No. The outside activity does not involve financial market data, trading algorithms, or any proprietary CME information. I will be working with standard, non-financial IT systems (e.g., legacy government databases) and will maintain strict physical and digital separation of all data."

**5. Reputational risk: Could outside activity result in reputational risk to CME Group?**
> **Draft Answer:** "No. The activity is strictly B2B (Business-to-Business) backend IT subcontracting. I am acting as an independent technical contractor. There is no public-facing brand, no consumer risk, and absolutely no association made with CME Group in my marketing or operations."

## Important Restrictions & IP Protection

## General Advice on Separation

**Hardware Separation:**
For your own peace of mind, it is always best practice to keep your side projects off your work laptop. By using your personal computer and your own private server going forward, you create a clean separation. It makes the HR approval process much smoother because you can confidently say "No company assets are used."

**Software Development Disclosure Form (IP Assignment):**
Almost all tech and finance companies have a standard clause asking you to assign IP if it is developed "in connection with your employment." This sounds intimidating, but it is just standard corporate boilerplate. They just want to make sure you aren't building a competing financial product.

Here is how you describe the software on that form:
> **Software Description:** "An independent, open-source-style script that parses and profiles standard SQLite/PostgreSQL database schemas for legacy IT migrations. It does not involve financial market data, high-frequency trading, or clearing house operations. All code was written 100% on personal time, using a personal computer, without the use of any CME hardware, software licenses, or proprietary knowledge."

**Why this works:** By explicitly stating what it *doesn't* do (trading/clearing), you make it very easy for CME's legal team to look at it, realize it poses zero threat to their IP, and approve it.
